This work surveys digital differential geometry: the discretization of smooth geometric objects (curves, surfaces, manifolds) into mesh-based representations amenable to computation. It covers discrete differential operators (gradient, divergence, Laplacian) on simplicial and cubical meshes, convergence properties as the mesh is refined, and the caveats that arise when discrete operators are substituted for their smooth counterparts — in particular, how boundary conditions, mesh quality, and coordinate choices affect numerical stability.

The second half outlines collaboration opportunities: applying these operators to physics simulation, computer graphics, and geometric deep learning, where the right discrete operators can serve as inductive biases that make learning from geometric data more sample-efficient.
