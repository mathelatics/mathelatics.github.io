The wave equation reduces, upon spatial discretization, to solving a large sparse linear system A x = b. We apply the Harrow–Hassidim–Lloyd (HHL) algorithm to this system, encoding the finite-difference wave operator as a Hermitian matrix and recovering |x〉 through quantum phase estimation, controlled rotation by inverse eigenvalues, and measurement. Implemented in Qiskit for small systems (up to 4 solution qubits), the circuit reproduces the classical finite-difference solution within shot noise. We discuss the state-preparation and readout overheads that determine whether the theoretical exponential speedup survives in practice.
