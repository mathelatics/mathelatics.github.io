This paper reconstructs how the standard number systems arise from the thirteen axioms of the real numbers ℝ. By selectively relaxing or replacing axioms — dropping completeness, removing commutativity, or extending the order — one obtains complex numbers, quaternions, p-adic numbers, and hyperreals. Each system is presented as an "axiomatic genome": a minimal set of mutations away from ℝ.

The paper surveys applications across physics (quaternions in rotation and quantum spin), robotics (slerp and spatial transformations), and cryptography (p-adic and finite-field arithmetic), showing how the same axiomatic toolkit underpins otherwise unrelated domains.
