# Research Areas

My research treats coherence as both a physical resource and a human one. Five active thrusts:

01

Fault-Tolerant Architectures

Surface & color codes, lattice surgery, and magic-state distillation with realistic noise models. Goal: reduce the overhead from logical qubit to useful circuit by 10×.

Surface CodesLattice SurgeryThreshold Theorems
02

Superconducting Qubit Hardware

Transmon design, 3D cavities, and parametric couplers. We chase T1/T2 beyond 500 µs while keeping fabrication yield above 90%.

TransmonsCircuit QEDCryogenics
03

Quantum Machine Learning

Barren-plateau avoidance, quantum kernels for materials discovery, and NISCA-era hybrid pipelines that actually beat classical baselines.

Variational AlgorithmsQuantum KernelsHybrid Pipelines
04

Open Quantum Software

Maintainer tooling for reproducible experiments: calibration daemons, schema'd pulse schedules, and simulation backends anyone can audit.

ReproducibilityPythonCUDA
05

Ethics, Policy & Inclusion in Quantum

Quantum technology governance, export-control equity, and workforce studies on LGBTQ+ retention in physics departments.

  1. 2026

    Logical Qubit Lifetime Beyond Break-Even in a Distance-7 Surface Code

    A. Rivera, J. Okafor, M. Lindqvist, et al.

    Nature Physics · doi:10.1038/s41567-026-0123-4

    #[error correction#experiment] ▲ 87 citations ▾ abstract

    We demonstrate a distance-7 surface code whose logical error rate falls exponentially with code distance, crossing the break-even threshold relative to the best physical qubit in the same device. Key improvements include tunable couplers with reduced crosstalk and a real-time MWPM decoder running at 1 MHz syndrome rates. Our results establish a practical path toward large-scale fault-tolerant computation.

  2. 2025

    Suppressing ZZ Crosstalk in Fixed-Frequency Transmon Arrays via Flux-Shunted Couplers

    M. Lindqvist, A. Rivera, T. Sandoval

    PRX Quantum · doi:10.1103/PRXQuantum.6.020301

    #[hardware#superconducting qubits] ▲ 142 citations ▾ abstract

    Static ZZ interactions remain a dominant error source in fixed-frequency transmon lattices. We introduce a flux-shunted coupler architecture that reduces residual ZZ by two orders of magnitude while preserving fast two-qubit gates, verified across a 60-qubit processor.

  3. 2024

    Real-Time Minimum-Weight Perfect Matching on FPGAs for Surface-Code Decoding

    J. Okafor, A. Rivera, S. Bhatt

    Quantum · doi:10.22331/q-2024-08-12-1456

    #[decoding#software#open source] ▲ 203 citations ▾ abstract

    We present a pipelined FPGA implementation of the minimum-weight perfect-matching decoder capable of processing distance-32 surface-code syndromes within a 1 µs cycle, enabling closed-loop active error correction. All firmware is open source.

# Open-Source Projects

Open-source work documented in Markdown under <code>src/content/projects/</code> — name, language, badge, stars and a full README-style description per project.

mwpm-fpga
decoding

Pipelined MWPM decoder firmware for real-time surface-code correction.

Streams syndromes at 1 MHz, matches up to distance-32 patches in under a microsecond, drop-in compatible with stim output. Used by four external groups to close the loop on active error correction experiments.

SystemVerilog 289

qnoise-kit
education

Teaching toolkit for noise channels, decoding demos, and rainbow-graded labs.

Jupyter-based lab exercises where students inject noise, run decoders, and plot logical error curves. Written for our "Quantum Error Correction for Everyone" workshop series; used by 12 universities.

Python 156

qublock
open hardware

Modular 16-channel analog control stack for superconducting qubits.

Full stack for lab-friendly qubit control: RF sources, baseband mixers, and a digital backplane. Includes schematics, Gerbers, firmware, and a calibration notebook template. Built because commercial racks were eating 60% of our grant money.
