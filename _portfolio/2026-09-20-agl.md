## Overview

AGL experiments with bringing algebraic geometry into neural networks: representing computations with ideals, Gröbner bases, and orthogonal polynomials so that a network's structure reflects algebraic relationships in the data rather than only dense matrix products.

## What I built

- **Algebraic layers:** layers that operate on polynomial ideals and use Gröbner-basis-style reductions as part of the forward computation.
- **Orthogonal polynomials:** polynomial bases (e.g., Chebyshev, Legendre) used as activation/function approximators within layers.
- **Integration:** a small framework connecting the algebraic layers to a standard training loop so they can be trained end to end with gradient descent.

## Outcome

The project is an exploration of whether inductive biases from algebraic geometry can make neural networks more sample-efficient and interpretable for problems with inherent polynomial structure — a research direction rooted in my Master's thesis on discrete differential geometry.
